I wrote several LinkedIn posts about how the hiring process feels from the candidate’s side. A lot of my frustration came from things a company can control, such as asking applicants to retype a résumé, leaving people without an update after several rounds, or setting requirements that don’t reflect the work.

## Make the interview resemble the job

If a role involves working with AI tools, explain whether a candidate can use them in the exercise and what you’re looking for when they do. A useful interview can show how someone thinks through a problem, handles errors, and checks their own work. Set expectations up front so candidates can focus on the exercise.

The same goes for the tone of the interview. By the time someone gets to a later round, they may have been through a lot of interviews and assessments, both with you and elsewhere. Giving them room to ask questions or take a moment to think can make the conversation more useful for both sides.

## Close the loop

A short decision email is preferable to silence. After someone has invested time in several interviews, even a little concrete feedback can help if the team is able to share it. Sending the message during normal working hours and avoiding holidays when practical are small things that show consideration.
