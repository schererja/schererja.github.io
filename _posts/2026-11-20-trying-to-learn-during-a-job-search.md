After our division was cut, I used part of the time to try AI tools, organize project ideas, and keep learning. I expected to get more time for personal projects. What I hadn’t accounted for was how much space a job search would take up, even when I was sitting down to do something else.

Applications and networking compete for the same attention as a side project. Then there’s deciding what to learn. Do I spend more time on the frontend, go deeper on backend work, or try another AI tool? At work, an actual problem often chooses the subject for you. I missed having that kind of focus.

Searching for roles could add to the frustration. A specific job title sometimes returned unrelated listings, and the search experience could vary from one session to the next. After spending time trying to find a role and getting little back, it was easy to feel like the day had disappeared.

I think it’s worth giving yourself room to work through that. Learn something because it’s interesting or because it answers a question you’ve actually run into. And if an opportunity feels like the wrong fit, it’s okay to move on.
